## 1. The occasion for this reflection

Presenting Paper 1 to an IS audience at UNNC, and then hearing Hu's lecture on modelling
customer-experience performance as a dynamic trajectory, did something a desk revision could
not: it let me see the paper from outside my own framing. Read back through the questions the
session raised, and through Hu's "trajectory + latent states + transition mechanisms" lens, the
scattered comments resolve into a single diagnosis, and that diagnosis is more useful to me than
any individual point. This document sets out that diagnosis, what I already think the paper does
well, the five changes I am committing to, and the order I will make them in.

## 2. What Paper 1 already does well (so I do not over-correct)

It is worth being clear about what should survive revision, because the risk after hard feedback
is to dismantle load-bearing walls.

- **A genuinely under-studied setting studied properly.** An embodied, VR-based social virtual
  world (Light World, via PICO headsets) is not where co-creation research has been built, and I
  have real, ethically-approved field access to it — roughly 90 hours of immersive netnography,
  457 transcribed public chat logs, an 8,000-word field diary, and ten interviews that
  deliberately reach newcomers, heavy contributors *and* users who have already left. The
  exited-user sample is an asset most co-creation papers do not have.
- **A trajectory, not a snapshot.** The paper already tells one developmental arc — from
  peripheral experiential participants, through prosumers, organisers and mentors, to committed
  members who turn critic and withdraw. That arc is the paper's spine and it is the right spine.
- **One authentically counter-intuitive finding.** *The most committed contributors become the
  platform's most influential critics.* This committed-to-critic conversion, together with the
  exhaustion of the unpaid emotional labour that organisers and mentors supply, is the paper's
  sharpest and most defensible contribution. Whatever else changes, this stays at the centre.

The problem is not the material. It is that the material currently *describes* more than it
*explains*.

## 3. The core diagnosis: from description to mechanism

Nearly every comment I received reduces to one root. In the presentation (and, honestly, in the
current draft) I state **what** happens — four activities and the value each yields, five roles,
five barriers — but I under-specify **how**: the process by which value is actually generated,
and the process by which it is eroded. "Users co-create relational value by socialising" names a
category and attaches an outcome to it; it does not show the mechanism that turns an avatar
gesture in a shared world into belonging, or the mechanism by which that belonging later drains
away. A reader can accept every one of my categories and still not know how the system works.

This single root explains four superficially different criticisms:

- *"Too surface-level; the deeper process of how value is generated is missing."* — This is the
  root stated directly.
- *"The metaverse is treated as a setting, not a theoretical provocation."* — A setting only
  matters descriptively; a mechanism is what makes the setting theoretically necessary.
- *"Is attenuation really a separate construct? The term has already been used; co-creation and
  co-destruction are one process."* — A construct defined by its symptoms (slow decline) invites
  this doubt; a construct defined by its mechanism does not.
- *"'Extending S-D logic into immersive environments' is a location, not a contribution."* — A
  location is a *where*; a contribution is a *how*.

So the revision is not eleven patches. It is one move — **push the whole paper from what to
how** — executed in five places. The five commitments below are that one move.

## 4. Five revision commitments

### 4.1 Make embodiment the mechanism, not the backdrop

Right now the paper says immersion *intensifies* and *amplifies* co-creation and its decline.
That is too weak — "amplify" still treats embodiment as a dial on a general process. The stronger
and truer claim is that embodiment **constitutes** the mechanism: it changes what the process is
made of. I will rebuild the front of the paper around three specific properties, each of which
does theoretical work that a text- or screen-based community cannot reproduce:

1. **Non-portability of built resources.** What a user constructs in Light World — a world, a
   shop, a gathering place — cannot be exported or carried elsewhere. The co-created resource is
   *locked to the platform*. This is not a nuance; it is why the stakes of platform failure are
   categorically higher than in a text community, where content is portable and identity travels.
   Non-portability turns ordinary participation into a sunk, un-recoverable investment, and that
   lock-in is a precondition for the committed-to-critic conversion.
2. **Removal of individual remedies.** In text-based communities the individual has private
   remedies — block, mute, scroll past, leave and take your following with you. Embodied
   spatial co-presence removes most of them: you cannot "scroll past" someone map-bombing the
   world you are standing in. When the individual remedy is gone, the *only* remedy is platform
   action — which is precisely why platform silence stops being merely disappointing and becomes
   **structural**. This reframes my "perceived unresponsiveness" barrier from a complaint into a
   mechanism.
3. **Spatial, felt depopulation.** Others' exit is not an abstract metric here; an emptying world
   is experienced spatially and bodily ("nobody plays anymore"). Decline is *perceived through
   presence*, which is why relational erosion propagates faster than in a feed.

Framed this way, the setting is no longer a backdrop I happen to study; it is the reason the
mechanism exists. This is also my answer to the conference's sharpest question — *what is
genuinely different about co-creation and its dark side in virtual environments?* The answer is
these three properties, not "it feels more immersive."

### 4.2 Foreground the process, not the categories

I will re-weight the findings and discussion so the organising object is the **process**, not
the taxonomy. Concretely:

- For value generation, I will consistently use the compositional mechanism the revised
  literature review already sets up — *which actors integrate which resources through which
  platform affordances to yield which value* — and actually trace it in the data, rather than
  labelling an activity and asserting its output. The coding tables give me the raw material; the
  prose currently under-uses it.
- For decline, I will answer the three process questions head-on: **when** each barrier emerges
  in the trajectory, **why** it emerges *then*, and **how** the barriers interact. My data already
  imply a recursive order — access narrows the creator base → fragile infrastructure devalues the
  labour of those who remain → depopulation drains organisers and mentors → toxicity raises the
  cost of presence → unresponsiveness converts voice into exit — but I currently present it as a
  sequence of categories rather than as a *compounding mechanism* in which each stage removes a
  resource the next role depended on. I will write it as the latter.

### 4.3 Reposition the central construct: attenuation as the bridge, not a rival

This is the point I have been most stuck on, and I now think I was stuck because I had framed the
question wrongly. I was defending "co-creation attenuation" as a **new standalone construct** that
sits *beside* value co-destruction. That framing is what draws every objection: the term has been
used before; co-creation and co-destruction are "really one process"; does it earn a separate
name at all? Defending a rival construct is a losing position.

The reframing: **attenuation is not a rival to co-destruction — it is the processual bridge that
shows co-creation and co-destruction are one continuous trajectory.** This does not weaken my
contribution; it *is* the contribution the discussion already gestures at ("the bright and dark
sides as one process rather than two"). Practically:

- **Lead with the mechanism, not the label.** What is new is not the word "attenuation"; it is
  (a) the ecosystem-level, resource-withdrawal mechanism — when creators leave, the knowledge
  mentor has no one left to mentor, so decline spreads through *interdependence*, not through any
  single dyadic breakdown; (b) the committed-to-critic conversion; and (c) the lock-in from
  non-portable resources (4.1). If a reviewer dislikes the term, the contribution still stands,
  because it lives in the mechanism.
- **Two modes, not one.** The conference was right that "attenuation" captures only the *slow,
  cumulative* mode. I will explicitly contrast it with a *fast, rupture* mode — a single
  catastrophic governance failure or a co-ordinated exit that collapses a world at a stroke.
  Naming both modes (slow erosion vs. abrupt rupture) does three things: it sharpens what I mean
  by the slow mode, it pre-empts the "but sometimes it crashes" objection, and it maps cleanly
  onto Hu's promotion/prevention asymmetry (prevention is exactly the work of keeping the slow
  mode from tipping into the fast one).
- **Engage the pointers directly.** I will locate the construct against Sahaym et al. (2023) and
  Tuunanen's work (suggested to me at and after the session) and inside the co-destruction
  literature (Plé and Chumpitaz Cáceres, 2010; Echeverri and Skålén, 2011, 2021), so that
  attenuation is positioned *within* an existing conversation rather than asserted as new
  vocabulary. Whether the final label stays "attenuation," shifts to a phrase that foregrounds the
  ecosystem/interdependence mechanism, or is demoted to a descriptor of a phase, is a decision I
  want to make with you — but the repositioning holds regardless of the word we choose.

### 4.4 Rewrite the discussion as contribution, not location

I will delete the sentences that state a *location* ("we extend S-D logic into immersive
environments," "co-creation is processual," "roles differ") and replace them with the *interplay*
they were standing in for. The three contributions become:

1. **Embodiment reconstitutes co-creation's resources and remedies** (from 4.1: non-portability,
   removal of individual remedies) — a mechanism, not a place.
2. **Co-creator roles as a layered, interdependent architecture with a reverse gear** — not a
   typology but a functional division of labour in which each role supplies a resource the next
   depends on, *and* in which the trajectory runs backwards when that support is withdrawn.
3. **Attenuation as the process that unifies the bright and dark sides** (from 4.3).

Each is phrased as a *how*, and each is tied to a specific piece of my data.

### 4.5 Deepen the qualitative work — and resist the pull to go quantitative too early

Two comments sit in tension and I want to be explicit about how I am resolving them. Hu's lecture
makes a quantitative, HMM-based operationalisation of exactly my trajectory look almost
tailor-made (and I develop that as a *future* paper — see the proposal document). But the
conference's advice was the opposite and, for *this* paper, correct: **do not rush into
quantitative method; make the qualitative work deep first.** If I bolt an HMM onto a mechanism I
have only described, I will have reproduced Paper 1's weakness in a more expensive form. So for
Paper 1 the commitment is to *depth*, not new methods:

- Deliver the full methodological transparency the last review round demanded and which the
  revised methodology now scaffolds — the audit trail from representative data → first-order
  codes → second-order themes → aggregate dimensions, the maximum-variation sample that includes
  exited users, the evidence-status labelling (N vs. I; verbatim vs. paraphrase), ethics-before-
  collection, and the reflexivity around single-coder, single-translator limitations. This was
  the main reason for the earlier reject and it is non-negotiable.
- Use that depth to *earn* the mechanism claims in 4.1–4.3, e.g. by process-tracing one or two
  users across the whole arc (entry → contribution → disillusion → exit) so the reader sees the
  compounding, not just the categories.

earns it) then 6.

## 6. Positioning and the one decision I need from you

The switch to *Online Information Review* and the de-retailing were the right call — the paper is
about co-creation and its erosion in an online/virtual community, and OIR's readership (online
communities, user participation, the dark side of engagement) is where the committed-to-critic
finding will land hardest. The mechanism reframing above pushes further in that direction, not
away from it.

The one thing I would value your steer on is **4.3 — the fate of the word "attenuation."** My
recommendation is to keep the term but subordinate it to the mechanism (ecosystem-level
resource-withdrawal; slow vs. rupture modes) so the paper never rests on having coined a
construct. But if you think the term is more liability than asset given that it has been used
elsewhere, I am ready to lead with a mechanism-first label instead. Either way the argument is
the same; only the signage changes.
