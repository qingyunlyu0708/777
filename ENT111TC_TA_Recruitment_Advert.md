# Teaching Assistant (TA) Work Plan & Justification — ENT111TC "AI Ideation and Strategy" Module (Semester 1)

## 1. Role Rationale and Workload Justification

This application seeks two (2) Teaching Assistants (TAs) to provide essential administrative and logistical support for the Year 2 ENT111TC "AI Ideation and Strategy" module — a Syntegrative, project-based module serving 257 students. The TAs are critical to ensuring the smooth operation of this large, practical-intensive module throughout the Semester 1 teaching cycle (Weeks 1–12). Working under the Module Leader (Dr. Jiyao Xun) and the teaching team, they will manage all non-pedagogical logistics, thereby allowing faculty to focus on teaching, coaching, and assessment.

The total required support is quantified as 80 hours, calculated as 2 TAs × 40 hours per TA = 80 hours, allocated across two main workstreams — In-Session Teaching Support and Event & Assessment Management. The detailed breakdown is as follows:

- **In-Session Practical Support (Weeks 1–7):** 6 practical sessions × 3 hours/session × 2 TAs = 36 hours. (Sessions run in Weeks 1–3 and 5–7; Week 4 is a University closure period for National Day. Each figure covers the scheduled session plus setup and pack-down.)
- **On-Site Presentation — Event Execution (Week 8):** 1 event × 5 hours/TA × 2 TAs = 10 hours.
- **On-Site Presentation — Pre-Event Logistics:** 5 hours × 2 TAs = 10 hours.
- **On-Site Presentation — Post-Event Administration:** 5 hours × 2 TAs = 10 hours.
- **Weekly Administration & Assessment Logistics:** 14 hours, for general weekly coordination and the management of 257 students' submissions before and after faculty marking.

## 2. Detailed Work Plan: In-Session Teaching Support (Weeks 1–7)

The primary role of the TAs during the teaching period is to act as operational facilitators for all live practical sessions. For the six practical sessions (Weeks 1–3 and 5–7; Week 4 is a University closure period), both TAs will be on duty per session to manage onsite logistics. This includes studio/room setup, AV system checks, managing student seating for a large cohort, distributing materials, supporting the use of AI and presentation tools, and assisting with technology to ensure smooth group discussions and presentations.

## 3. Detailed Work Plan: On-Site Presentation Event & Post-Event Management (Week 8)

The Week 8 On-Site Presentation represents the peak logistical demand, requiring dedicated TA support across three phases:

- **Pre-Event Preparation (10 hours):** TAs will assist with confirming venue bookings, coordinating invited evaluators/industry guests and preparing their folders and score sheets, decoration planning, and coordinating the presentation schedule across the student groups.
- **On-Site Event Execution (10 hours):** On the event day, both TAs will provide dedicated on-site support. Responsibilities include final venue setup and decoration, welcoming and directing evaluators and students, managing AV and video recording of all presentations, strict timekeeping, collecting evaluators' score sheets, troubleshooting technical issues, and managing venue exit and breakdown.
- **Post-Event Administration (10 hours):** This critical phase involves processing all materials from the event. TAs will systematically collect, scan, and archive all physical score/marking sheets; manage the post-production of the video recordings (editing, labeling, and uploading files to the Learning Mall for moderation); and perform accurate data entry of all presentation scores from the student groups into the master grade spreadsheet.

## 4. Detailed Work Plan: General Administration & Assessment Logistics

Beyond the event, TAs provide ongoing support, including weekly coordination, communications, and Learning Mall updates. For assessment, TAs manage the logistics for the 257 students' Task 1 submissions (group project portfolio reports and individual peer-review forms) on the Learning Mall — handling the bulk export, anonymization and organization for faculty marking, and the subsequent compilation, assortment, and secure storage of all scripts after marking is complete.

## 5. Summary of TA Requirements

To fulfill this operational work plan, successful candidates must be exceptionally organized, proactive, and possess strong communication skills. Proficiency in AV equipment, video recording, data management in Excel, and familiarity with the Learning Mall system are essential; familiarity with common AI tools is an advantage given the module's subject matter. Given the need for intensive in-person support, preference will be given to candidates based on or near the Taicang (XEC) campus. Current doctoral (PhD) students are ideally suited to this role, as it provides direct insight into large-scale academic event and module administration, although strong Master's students with relevant experience are also encouraged to apply. Experience in event coordination, AV support, or prior administrative roles is highly desirable.
