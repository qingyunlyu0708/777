## 4.3 Research Sites, Sampling and Data (the routes)

**Sampling logic.** Section 1 located the study in metaverse brand virtual stores; this section selects specific sites within that field. Site selection follows the sampling canon of netnography itself rather than that of multiple-case research. Kozinets's criteria for choosing online fieldsites, namely relevance, activity, interactivity, substantiality, heterogeneity and richness of data (Kozinets, 2010, 2020), direct the researcher towards settings in which the phenomenon is currently alive and observable in depth, and their adoption here aligns the sampling logic with the fieldwork tradition from which the method derives. The decisive consideration is epistemic rather than administrative: the generative engine of this study is sustained first-person immersion, and constructs generated from thin or dormant sites would be underdetermined by data. Sites are therefore selected for the richness of experience they can yield, and ecological contrast enters the design as variation across the selected portfolio rather than as a criterion that overrides data richness.

**Eligibility criteria.** A store enters the candidate pool only if it satisfies four operational criteria: it supports the complete loop from browsing to transaction within the experience; it shows evidence of active maintenance, operationalised as a content update or brand activity within the preceding ninety days; it offers content depth sufficient to sustain a first visit of at least thirty minutes; and it is accessible to the researcher with an ordinary consumer configuration. Where a platform publishes concurrent visitor counts, a minimum level of ongoing visitation is applied as a further screen. These criteria operationalise Kozinets's activity, substantiality and data-richness requirements for the specific field of brand virtual stores.

**Pre-fieldwork site audit.** Selection is preceded by a structured audit lasting two to three weeks, in which fifteen to twenty candidate stores across platforms are each visited at least once and assessed against the eligibility criteria. The audit is recorded in a standardised table (Appendix A) covering platform, brand, operational status, loop completeness, content depth and visitation, so that the final selection rests on documented evidence rather than convenience. The audit table also records stores that fail the criteria, including abandoned and low-content spaces; documenting these exclusions preserves the state of the field at the time of study and makes the boundary of the study's empirical claims explicit.

**Portfolio composition.** From the eligible pool, four to six stores are selected to maximise variation across the four dimensions of platform ecology specified in Section 2.4, namely economic structure, governance rules, demographic composition and richness of embodiment. An illustrative composition is a flagship brand world on Roblox of the Nikeland type; a Roblox store employing the physical-goods commerce integration introduced in 2025, in which virtual and physical purchase loops coexist; a brand island on Fortnite; a brand space on Zepeto; and a brand-owned web-based 3D store. The sampling is purposive rather than representative: the aim is maximum variation for theory building (Patton, 2002), not coverage of the market. The contrast between luxury and mass brands is realised within the portfolio, where possible within a single platform, and brand category is carried as a coded property of every fragment, so that category effects can be separated analytically from ecology effects. Where one brand operates spaces in more than one selected ecology, the overlap is exploited for a controlled comparison, but the design does not depend on any such overlap existing.

**Pre-declared inference rules.** Four rules, declared before fieldwork, govern how cross-site patterns are read. A mechanism that recurs across sites differing on an ecology dimension is a candidate general mechanism of embodied commerce with respect to that dimension. A mechanism that recurs across brands within one ecology is robust to brand category within that ecology. A mechanism confined to a single store is treated as a configurational boundary condition and output as a testable proposition. A systematic contrast between two sites that differ chiefly on one ecology dimension is read as evidence of that dimension's conditioning effect, within the limits of a non-experimental design. A platform ecology memo is maintained for every site, recording platform version, economic structure, demographic composition and governance rules as they stand during fieldwork, and is treated as data.

**Duration and scale.** Each selected store is visited six to ten times, each visit lasting forty-five to ninety minutes, across a fieldwork period of three to four months. The expected corpus is thirty to fifty thousand words of field notes and several hundred codable journey fragments and friction events, which supplies the comparative base required by the shift of the unit of analysis defended in Section 4.1.

**Data streams.** Six data streams are collected.

First, immediate reflexive field notes constitute the core data, written to the structured fragment template specified in Section 4.4.

Second, screen recordings anchor the narrative visually and yield behavioural trace indicators, including dwell time per touchpoint, retry and hesitation counts, task completion time, and teleport and abandonment events.

Third, a lightweight momentary self-rating, one single-item experience and presence score per fragment (Bouchard et al., 2004), accumulates into a longitudinal timeline.

Fourth, public user discourse about the same stores, drawn from platform reviews and public Reddit and Discord threads, is archived for the resonance check described in Section 4.1, with anonymised quotation and no interaction.

Fifth, a methodological reflexive journal is kept separately, as specified in Section 4.4.

Sixth, the consumer validation layer comprises four to six adult consumers, balanced between metaverse novices and experienced users, each completing one natural visit of forty-five to sixty minutes with full screen recording, followed within twenty-four hours by a video-cued retrospective interview (Lahlou, 2011). This layer is scheduled late in the fieldwork, after a preliminary typology has formed, and its function is validation and extension rather than generation: it tests whether the touchpoint types and friction configurations generated by the researcher recur in other journeys, and it captures frictions that lie outside the researcher's positionality, for instance those met by users without theoretical training or prior VR experience.

**Position of the quantitative embedding.** The behavioural traces and the rating timeline serve two functions only. As a fragment sampler, they select targets for deep description by explicit rules, namely extreme fragments supplemented by theoretical sampling, with the rules recorded in the audit trail to guard against selective quotation. As triangulation, divergence between recorded behaviour and narrated experience, for instance a note reporting fluency while the recording shows four retries, is treated as analytic material in its own right. This use of analytic metrics inside an auto-netnography follows Villegas (2018), and the treatment of counts derived from qualitative material follows the quantitizing literature (Sandelowski, Voils & Knafl, 2009; Fetters, Curry & Creswell, 2013). No statistical inference is drawn from these data.

## 4.4 Analytic Rigour Protocol (the map)

On the evocative to analytic spectrum of autoethnographic work, this study is explicitly analytic, and Anderson's (2006) five criteria serve as its quality framework: complete member researcher status; analytic reflexivity; visibility of the researcher in the text; dialogue with data beyond the self, satisfied here through the consumer validation layer and the public discourse archive; and commitment to theoretical analysis. To make the analytic stance auditable rather than merely intended, six discipline devices are built into the fieldwork routine.

First, a dual ledger separates evocative field notes, which are data, emic and permitted to be emotional, from analytic memos, which are etic; the two are kept in physically separate documents and never mixed. Second, every field note follows a structured fragment template with fixed fields for situation, event, immediate emotion, behavioural response and initial interpretation. Third, coding is delayed: at least one week separates the writing of a fragment from its coding, manufacturing analytic distance within a contemporaneous design. Fourth, collaborative interrogation follows the collaborative variant of auto-netnography (Ashman, Patterson & Kozinets, 2021): fieldwork is conducted alone, but supervisors and collaborators act as a theoretically robust sounding board in scheduled devil's advocate meetings, whose minutes enter the audit trail. Fifth, a disconfirming-evidence discipline requires every coding round to include an explicit search for fragments that contradict emerging themes, and the results are processed in memos. Sixth, a methodological reflexive journal, kept apart from the field notes, records researcher identity states, emotional baseline, technical conditions and platform version changes, and is reflected upon systematically in the discussion chapter.

## 4.5 Data Analysis (the learning)

Of the three levels of learning distinguished by Villegas (2018), this study aims principally at theory, that is, at construct and mechanism contributions; learning at the level of brand and organisation is retained as a secondary output, and practitioner-level lessons are collected in the reflexive journal.

Analysis proceeds as abductive thematic analysis in three passes. Open coding marks journey stages, touchpoint forms and friction events. Axial coding links frictions to touchpoints and stages, and codes configurations across touchpoint type, platform resource and experience dimension. Selective coding brings the emerging structure into dialogue with the TCQ and embodiment literatures and with the asymmetry lineage reviewed in Section 2.3.

The codebook enforces the dual-track logic of Section 2.3. Every friction event is first coded on the five antecedent properties, namely intensity, locus, attribution, reparability and design intent, and its journey outcome is coded independently as dwelling, comparing, confirming, rerouting, interruption or abandonment. Good and bad friction are not codes but a mapping to be discovered: which property configurations correspond systematically to constructive outcomes and which to destructive ones is answered by cross-event comparison, so that the distinction drawn by Towers and Towers (2025) functions as a falsifiable hypothesis space, and cases in which the same configuration leads to opposite outcomes in different contexts are compulsorily entered into the disconfirming memos. Promotion and prevention labels are retained as abductive hypothesis tags grounded in regulatory focus theory (Higgins, 1997), without presuming that the firm-level asymmetry reported by Hong and Hu (2025) holds at the level of individual journeys.

Data from the consumer validation layer are coded independently with the same codebook, and both recurrences and novel types are recorded in the audit trail. Cross-site comparison follows the inference rules declared in Section 4.3 and yields output in the form of mechanisms together with boundary-condition propositions. Quality is assessed by narrative coherence, researcher credibility and reader resonance (Ellis, Adams & Bochner, 2011), supported by a complete audit trail.

## 4.6 Writing and Reporting Strategy (the telling)

The findings will be written in a register between the impressionist and realist tales of van Maanen (1988), keeping story and theory in direct conversation, which Villegas (2018) recommends for auto-netnography aimed at journal publication. The findings chapter is organised on the theoretical framework rather than on chronology, following the structural strategy of Ashman, Patterson and Kozinets (2021): journey forms first (RQ1), then the transformation of the nine building blocks and the conceptualisation of embodied touchpoints (RQ2), then the mapping from friction properties to journey outcomes (RQ3). Field-note material enters the text only as data excerpts embedded in this analytic frame, and no part of the paper adopts a memoir register. The double structure announced at the head of this chapter, a study organised as a journey being used to study a consumption journey, is carried through the paper as a connecting thread.

## 4.7 Ethics and Researcher Care (the safety of the trip)

The core data are the researcher's own experiences. Observation is confined to public commercial spaces; no private information about other users is collected; avatars of other users appearing incidentally in screen recordings are blurred before analysis; and public discourse is quoted only in anonymised and non-traceable form, in line with the principle of doing no harm (Ellis, 2007). Because Roblox hosts many under-age users, two additional safeguards apply there: the researcher does not interact with users who may be minors, and no content that could identify a minor is quoted.

The consumer validation layer constitutes research with human participants and will be submitted for ethics review under the university's standard procedure. Only adults are recruited; informed consent covers both screen recording and interview audio; data are stored in encrypted form; and reporting is anonymised. For the researcher's own experiences and the observation of public spaces, the applicable ethical framework will be confirmed with the department before fieldwork begins.

Finally, the design takes seriously Villegas's (2018) element of taking care of the self. Frustration and fatigue during months of immersive fieldwork are simultaneously data and risk: they are monitored through the reflexive journal, and an upper limit is set on the duration of any single fieldwork session.
