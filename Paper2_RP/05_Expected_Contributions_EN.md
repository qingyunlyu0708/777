# 5. Expected Contributions

**Primary contribution: the embodied touchpoint construct and its friction mechanism account.** The study proposes embodied touchpoints, distinguished from interface-event touchpoints by the criteria of locus, temporality and enactment (Section 2.2), in direct dialogue with the TCQ tradition (De Keyser et al., 2020; Hong & Hu, 2025). The construct answers the ontological question of what a touchpoint is in a digitally embodied environment, and the digital body as an operant resource connects it to service-dominant logic (Vargo & Lusch, 2008). Its value is to be cashed out as explanatory power (Suddaby, 2010; MacInnis, 2011): the property-to-outcome analysis of RQ3 is designed to show that the same obstacle produces opposite journey outcomes under different embodied configurations, a phenomenon the interface-event conception cannot express because friction there is encountered rather than enacted. The mechanism account is therefore not a second contribution but the construct's proof, and it simultaneously moves metaverse barrier research from variable lists towards a configurational and, as an empirical question, asymmetric mechanism theory.

**Subordinate contribution: platform ecology as boundary condition.** Applying the pre-declared inference rules to the heterogeneous multi-site portfolio (Section 4.3) yields propositions of the form mechanism plus boundary condition, which theorise platform ecology as the Context dimension of the TCQ framework and respond to the research agenda of Yoo et al. (2023).

**Claims explicitly not made.** The distinctions between good and bad friction and between promotion and prevention are analytic devices inside the primary contribution, not contributions in their own right. The methodological protocols developed for the study, namely the discipline devices, the quantitative sampling apparatus and the double-journey writing structure, do not occupy contribution space here; they are planned as a separate methodological paper responding to Kozinets's (2023) call for method innovation in immersive environments, and appear in this study only as appendix material supporting auditability.
