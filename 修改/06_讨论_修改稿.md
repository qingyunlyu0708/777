Our findings tell a single story. In a social virtual world (SVW), platform affordances and
user resources enable interaction activities that co-create distinct forms of value and
configure users into co-creator roles along a participation trajectory; the same trajectory,
when structural conditions fail, reverses into withdrawal. On this basis we develop three
contributions to research on online communities and value co-creation.

## 6.1 Value co-creation as spatially embodied and place-based

First, the study extends value co-creation beyond the text- and screen-based practices on
which it has largely been theorised (Schau et al., 2009). Whereas prior accounts cast
co-creation as posting, sharing and commenting, our participants co-create by moving through,
dwelling in and building persistent worlds, so that value is anchored in embodied presence and
attachment to place rather than in textual exchange (Belk, 2013; Kozinets, 2023). This is not
a cosmetic upgrade of familiar practices: immersion and spatial co-presence reorganise how
value is produced, which implies that findings from screen-based communities cannot be assumed
to transfer directly to embodied ones (Suh, 2024; Yang and Han, 2021). For research on online
information environments, this repositions immersion as a substantive condition of
participation, not merely a richer interface.

## 6.2 Co-creator roles as evolving configurations within a layered participation architecture

Second, we reconceptualise co-creator roles as configurations of resource integration that
evolve along a trajectory, and show how they compose a layered participation architecture.
Existing typologies tend to be static and screen-based (Kolyperas et al., 2019; Xu et al.,
2025), and even work that recognises movement emphasises only the upward path from lurker to
contributor (Chapman and Dilmperi, 2022; Lave and Wenger, 1991). We extend this in two ways.
First, value is produced not by any single role but by a layered architecture in which
differentiated roles perform complementary functions, experiential participants supplying
attention, prosumers content, organisers traffic, mentors onboarding and critics governance
signals, so that community value rests on their interdependence rather than on individual
engagement intensity (Brodie et al., 2011). Second, sustaining this architecture depends on
emotional labour: organisers and mentors perform substantial, largely unpaid affective work
(Hochschild, 1983), which generates relational value while the community thrives but tips into
exhaustion when technical and governance support fail. The architecture is therefore robust
and fragile at once, an underexamined vulnerability that engagement-intensity accounts of
participation tend to miss (Dessart et al., 2015).

## 6.3 Co-creation attenuation as a processual, ecosystem-level mechanism

Third, and centrally, we introduce co-creation attenuation. Unlike value co-destruction, which
denotes discrete, dyadic episodes of diminished well-being (Plé and Chumpitaz Cáceres, 2010;
Echeverri and Skålén, 2011), and unlike individual-level fatigue, disengagement or
platform decay (Nadeem et al., 2025b), attenuation is a gradual, cumulative and
ecosystem-level erosion produced by the recursive misalignment of access, infrastructure,
relational, normative and governance conditions; it can precede and, at its extreme, give way
to co-destruction. Its engine is a counterintuitive reversal. The platform's most invested
members, not its casual users, become its most consequential critics: having exhausted voice,
they turn to exit and, crucially, discourage others (Hirschman, 1970). Because their judgements
carry the authority of heavy investment, committed users turning critic shape newcomers'
perceptions and mobilise collective sentiment, so that the very engagement that built the
community accelerates its decline. This overturns the common assumption that highly engaged
users are unconditionally beneficial, and identifies engagement as simultaneously the principal
source of co-created value and of its vulnerability.

Across the three contributions one mechanism recurs: the immersive, embodied features that make
co-creation in SVWs especially rich are the same features that magnify the cost of technical
and governance failure, binding value and vulnerability into a single trajectory rather than
treating the bright and dark sides of engagement as separate phenomena.
