The same engagement that builds a community's value can, over time, produce the conditions for its decline — and its most invested members can become its most consequential critics.
Motivation Where the literature stops Research gap

Co-creation is theorised as a one-way climb. Decline is treated as a separate accident.

The optimistic account
Service-dominant logic casts users as active co-creators; online-community research assumes deeper participation cumulatively enhances value, drawing members from periphery to centre.
The negative side — but fragmented
Value co-destruction, the dark side of engagement, fatigue & platform decline each capture decline as discrete failures or individual withdrawal — detached from the value-creating process they emerge from.
The gap: how creation and erosion are linked within a single trajectory of participation — and how immersive, embodied VR intensifies both.
Research questions One cumulative mechanism, three linked questions
  • RQ1 · Activities → value. Through what interaction activities do users co-create value in an immersive social virtual world, and what forms of value do these activities generate?
  • RQ2 · Roles. How do these activities configure different users into distinct co-creator roles?
  • RQ3 · Attenuation. How do structural barriers progressively attenuate co-creation across these roles and over time?
The logic is cumulative: activities create value → participation crystallises into roles → barriers withdraw the conditions that sustain them.
Theory A compositional, sociotechnical reading of S-D logic

Value is not embedded in outputs but co-created in use, when actors integrate resources through platform affordances (Vargo & Lusch, 2004, 2008).

We move beyond "users are resource integrators" to ask the compositional question:

which resources, through which affordances, by which actors, generate which forms of value?
Sociotechnical spine
User resources — skill, creativity, social ties, emotional labour, time, attention — meet platform resources — spatial infrastructure, creation tools, avatar systems, real-time voice, governance.
Co-creation lives at the intersection of the social and the technical — neither alone.
Building co-creation
Method Research design & a revelatory site
Abductive qualitative design
  • Iterating between data and theory (Dubois & Gadde, 2002) — categories emerge from data, refined against co-creation theory.
  • Two sources triangulated: immersive netnography + interviews.
Light World — why this site
  • VR-based SVW on PICO headsets: avatar-mediated, user-built persistent worlds, spatial voice; ~10–16 co-present per world.
  • Chosen as a revelatory, not representative, case (Patton, 2015): it made the full arc — including decline — observable in one setting.
A site selected precisely because it lets us watch co-creation both build and erode — the aim was a revelatory case, not a typical one.

The pivot Where the trajectory turns Most consequential finding
Engagement is value and vulnerability

The critic role is typically the destination of the trajectory — not an outside disruptor.

Many critics began as committed creators and organisers, and turned only after repeated, unaddressed barriers. Because they invested most, they are the most credible critics when governance fails — and their disillusioned discourse colours newcomers and mobilises others toward exit.

Two sub-mechanisms
1 · Committed → critic conversion. The most invested don't leave quietly; they reshape the community's reputation.
2 · Emotional-labour exhaustion. The unpaid warmth of organisers & mentors tips into burnout once technical & governance support falls away.
This overturns a common assumption: the most engaged users are not unconditionally beneficial.
Finding 3 · RQ3 Five structural barriers — a recursive sequence, not a list
1
Access & participation constraints — VR-only reach + a complex toolkit narrow the creator base at the source.
2
Fragile interaction infrastructure — lag, crashes, logic failures make creative labour feel wasted.
3
Relational & emotional erosion — thinning content drives audiences away; emotional labour turns to exhaustion.
4
Normative breakdown — thinned, weakly-governed spaces fill with map bombing & abuse.
5
Perceived platform unresponsiveness — official silence converts voice into exit, and private grievance into public criticism.
Recursive, not parallel: each barrier seeds the next — a sociotechnical cascade from a technical fault to social erosion to governance failure. We name this cumulative erosion co-creation attenuation.

Implications Decline is manageable, not inevitable
  • Read the early markers. Creators quitting, public spaces emptying, reports going unanswered — treat attenuation as detectable and manageable.
  • Responsiveness is the highest-leverage lever. Because unresponsiveness closes the recursive loop, visible action forestalls the committed-to-critic conversion.
  • Protect the emotional-labour roles. Organisers & mentors hold communities together through unpaid labour — shielding them from burnout is both ethical and strategic.
  • Govern role by role. Different roles need different support — from stable cross-device creation tools to transparent, visible feedback loops.
The practical question is not how to transact, but how to sustain co-creation over time — a continuous, sociotechnical task across access, infrastructure, relationships and governance.
In one line …and where I'd value your guidance
A community does not simply accumulate value or, separately, suffer failure. It builds co-creative vitality — and then loses it along the very trajectory that created it.
